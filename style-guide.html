01

What makes it feel the way it does.

This design reads as a trusted public institution rather than a brochure, and it does that through restraint. Four mechanisms carry almost all of the feeling.

  • Near-square corners at 2px. Not sharp, not rounded. Rounded corners read as consumer software; sharp corners read as a legal notice. Two pixels sits between them and reads as printed matter.
  • No shadows at all. Nothing floats. Every edge is either a one-pixel hairline or the natural boundary of a photograph. Flatness is what keeps it from looking like a template.
  • Opposing letter-spacing. Tiny uppercase labels are stretched wide at .2em. Large headlines are pulled tight at -.032em. That tension between the two is the single most recognisable move in the system.
  • Photography does the emotional work. The palette is almost monochrome, so colour arrives only through the landscape. The design gets out of the way of the place.

The emotional target is stewardship. Quiet, careful, permanent. Anything that reads as urgent, promotional or clever is off-brand here, because this is a District that holds land in trust, not a business selling something.

02

Layout system.

One container, one gutter, one vertical rhythm. Pages differ by which blocks they use, never by how wide or how padded they are.

  • Container. 1320px maximum width, centred, with a fluid side gutter of clamp(20px, 4vw, 40px). Nothing on the site sits outside this except full-bleed photo sections.
  • Vertical rhythm. Standard section padding is clamp(58px, 8vw, 116px) top and bottom. Sections that butt against a dark panel drop their adjoining padding to zero so the panel touches the section above it.
  • The section right after a hero or a data strip takes section--tight-top, which drops the top padding to clamp(44px, 5.5vw, 86px). Full padding there leaves a hole, because the strip already carries its own bottom padding.
  • Column behaviour. Everything is flexbox with flex-wrap and a basis in pixels. Columns reflow on their own when the space runs out, and there are no width breakpoints to maintain.
  • One exception: the archive lists use CSS grid (repeat(auto-fill, minmax(250px, 1fr))). A wrapping flex row lets the last partial row stretch, which knocks the final few entries of each year out of column. Grid keeps them aligned.
  • Line length is capped in characters. Body copy stops at 46ch, list rows at 58ch, headlines between 17ch and 22ch. This is why nothing sprawls on a wide monitor.
  • Above the fold. One photograph, one headline of ten words or fewer, one supporting sentence, one action. Never two competing actions.

The page skeleton

Every interior page is assembled from this order. Drop any block that has nothing to say, but never reorder them.

  • 1. Header. Identical site-wide.
  • 2. Subhero. Photo, label, page headline, one sentence, one button.
  • 3. Data strip. Two to four hard facts on hairline-topped columns.
  • 4. Body blocks. Splits, card grids, rate lists or row lists, in whatever order the content needs.
  • 5. Dark panel. Optional, for official or District-level content.
  • 6. Closer. Full-bleed photo, centred question, phone and email.
  • 7. Footer. Identical site-wide.

The warm tan from the earlier brand direction (#c9ae77) is deliberately absent. Adding a second accent breaks the near-monochrome logic and makes the landscape photography compete instead of lead. If tan is ever needed for print, keep it out of the web palette.

Photo scrims

White text only sits on a photograph through a bottom-up dark wash. The wash is always built from rgba(10, 13, 8, x), a near-black with a green cast that matches the ink.

  • Hero. .86 at the base, holding .24 at the top, so a tall image stays readable throughout.
  • Cards. .86 at the base falling to .10 by 82 percent, so most of the photo stays clean.
  • Full-bleed closer. A flat rgba(8, 10, 6, .66) across the whole image, because the text is centred rather than sitting at the bottom.
04

Typography.

Schibsted Grotesk at three weights. Hierarchy comes from size and letter-spacing, never from adding another typeface.

  • 500 is the workhorse. Every headline uses it. Large type at a medium weight reads as editorial; large type at bold reads as advertising.
  • 600 is for small emphasis. Labels, buttons, row titles, names.
  • 700 appears three times on the whole site. The wordmark and the notice label. Nothing else.
  • Numbers use tabular figures so prices, dates and statistics align in columns.

05

Imagery.

Full colour, never filtered, always local. The photography is the only place colour enters the design, so it has to carry weight.

  • Subject. Real Christmas Valley: the landscape, the facilities, and people using them. No stock photography, ever. A slightly imperfect local photo beats a polished generic one every time.
  • Treatment. No filters, no duotone, no desaturation. The high-desert light already does the work.
  • Framing. object-fit: cover with an explicit object-position tuned per image so the subject survives cropping at every width.
  • Credit. Community photographs are named in the caption and in the footer. That attribution is part of the brand, not a formality.
  • Weight. Resize to 2400px on the long edge before committing. Rural connections are the constraint that matters here.

07

Build rules.

Four rules keep fifteen pages consistent without anyone policing them.

  • Never type a colour, size or spacing value into a page. Every value lives in css/site.css as a named token. If a page needs a value the system does not have, add a token rather than an inline style.
  • Start every new page by copying _page-template.html. Delete the blocks you do not need. Never start from a finished page, because you will inherit its content decisions.
  • Header and footer are never edited per page. The only per-page change is adding aria-current="page" to the active nav link.
  • No JavaScript unless a page genuinely cannot work without it. The archive needs it. Nothing else does. Pages that work without JavaScript stay fast and never break silently.

Starting a new page

cp _page-template.html golf.html
# then, in golf.html:
#   1. set the title, description and og:image
#   2. swap images/REPLACE.jpg for the real photographs
#   3. delete every block the page does not need
#   4. add aria-current="page" to the matching nav link

Accessibility floor

  • Every image needs real alt text describing what is in it, not the filename.
  • The skip link stays at the top of every page.
  • Focus rings are forest green at 2px with 3px offset. Never remove them.
  • All motion is disabled under prefers-reduced-motion. Do not add animation that ignores it.
08

Adapting it, and what not to copy.

This system belongs to the District. The thinking behind it travels; the specifics do not.

Worth carrying anywhere
  • The 2px radius and the refusal to use shadows.
  • Wide tracking on small caps against tight tracking on large display type.
  • Capping measure in ch units rather than pixels.
  • One accent colour, earning its place by being rare.
  • Fluid clamp() sizing instead of breakpoints.
Belongs to this District only
  • The exact greens and sage greys. Another client needs their own.
  • Schibsted Grotesk as the single face.
  • The high-desert photography and the photographer credits.
  • The stewardship voice. A retail client would sound evasive using it.
  • The specific section order, which follows what this District's visitors ask for.

To reuse the formula for another client, keep the structure of site.css and replace the values in the token block at the top. The component classes below it will keep working. That token block is the portable part; everything under it is just plumbing.
